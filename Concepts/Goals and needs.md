**The Essence:** The driving force behind user behavior. If the profile is the "who," the Goals and Needs are the **"What for?"** and **"What's missing?"**

---

## Defining the Duo

|**Feature**|**Goals (Objectives)**|**Needs (Requirements)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Focus**|Specific outcomes or milestones.|Essential information or support required.|
|**Example**|"Lose 5kg in two months" (Barua et al.)|"Find a healthy recipe" (Horvitz et al.)|
|**Duration**|Usually long-term or terminal.|Contextual and immediate.|
|**Modeling Method**|Causal models, Personality nodes.|Bayesian inference, Query analysis.|

---

## Evolution of the Research

The way we understand these concepts has shifted from static checkboxes to complex, "living" models:

- **The Bayesian Approach (1998):** Early systems used background data and current actions to "guess" what a user wanted next to provide tailored help.
    
- **The Causal Approach (2003):** Linking **personality traits** to goals. For example, a "competitive" personality node might trigger a goal to "win the game" in an educational setting.
    
- **The Dynamic Approach (2022):** The **NEST framework** recognizes that extreme events (like a global pandemic) can cause a "Need Evolution," where a user's baseline requirements shift overnight.

## How They Are Modeled (A Quick "Map")

According to the research, inferring these requires a mix of three data streams:

1. **Historical Background:** (Static) Who they were when they signed up.
    
2. **Current Actions:** (Dynamic) What they are clicking right now.
    
3. **Psychological Theory:** (Framework) Using "Goal Model" representations to facilitate self-reflection.
    

> **Key Takeaway:** A goal is the **destination** (e.g., health), while a need is the **fuel** (e.g., pertinent information) required to get there.

## 9.10 Digital Twin for Electricity Distribution Network with Distributed Generation and Flexible Load (EN_33)

The technical enablers within T4.1 focus on the virtualization of the distribution network and the creation of interfaces for multi-purpose visualization. The digital twin for the electricity distribution network serves as the foundational analytical engine, facilitating the missing data replacement (REQ_UC4.1) requirement. By utilizing state estimation algorithms and historical baseline datasets, the digital twin synthesizes virtual sensor data to maintain operational continuity in the event of telemetry loss. Furthermore, the twin's architecture is optimized for high-frequency synchronization to satisfy data availability (REQ_UC4.2) constraints. This ensures that 15-minute energy usage packets are processed and made available within the strict latency "hard limits" required for near real-time grid state projection.

## 9.11 Dashboard for Energy Community Management (EN_34)

Adding to the virtual modeling, a dashboard for energy community management serves as the primary gateway for human oversight. This enabler fulfills the energy community management requirement (REQ_UC4.7) through a robust UI and a series of REST API calls designed to handle the lifecycle of community participants. It integrates the energy community overview (REQ_UC4.13) and energy community import/export (REQ_UC4.14) requirements by aggregating high-dimensional IoT data into simplified visual KPIs. These visualizations allow operators to monitor production and consumption trends at both the prosumer and community levels, providing the necessary transparency to validate the full potential of community-based energy services.

## 9.12 Activation of Flexible Resources, Like Storage and Electric Vehicles (EN_35)

Technical enablers, related to T4.3, address the technical requirements for localized, real-time actuation through the activation of flexible resources, specifically targeting battery energy storage systems and electric vehicle integration. This enabler is intrinsically linked to the IoT data retrieval (REQ_UC4.10) requirement, as autonomous decision-making necessitates the acquisition of status points from a heterogeneous array of participating IoT devices. The R&D in this task focuses on low-latency communication protocols that allow the system to autonomously dispatch flexible loads to balance the local grid without requiring centralized cloud intervention, thereby enhancing the resilience of the community microgrid.

## 9.13 Autonomous Detection and Mitigation of Voltage Issues (EN_36)

Technical enablers, related to T4.4, focus on the situational awareness frameworks capable of distinguishing between nominal grid states and emergency conditions. The enabler for the detection and responses functions as a closed-loop control system that monitors the energy community import/export data. By applying automated reasoning to detect voltage deviations, the system triggers corrective actions to keep the grid within safe operational bounds. This enabler bridges the gap between passive monitoring and active grid stabilization, ensuring that the community's net energy exchange remains harmonized with the Distribution System Operator (DSO) specifications while moving the local cluster toward its targeted energy-efficiency state.

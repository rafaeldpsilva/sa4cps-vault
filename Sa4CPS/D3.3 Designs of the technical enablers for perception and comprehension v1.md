#### _**Autonomous detection and mitigation of voltage issues**_ _**(ISEP)**_

The autonomous detection and mitigation of voltage issues enabler provides a situational awareness framework that continuously monitors the electricity distribution network to distinguish between nominal grid states and emergency conditions. Its primary role in the use case is the early detection and prevention of anomalies such as voltage deviations and network overloads, thereby supporting disaster prevention within the energy community infrastructure.
The perception mechanism relies on smart meters, voltage, current, and frequency, and edge monitoring systems deployed at strategic points across the distribution network. These devices provide the raw electrical measurements required to assess the real-time state of the grid.
From an architectural standpoint, the system comprises monitoring nodes located in close proximity to the data sources, connected to a centralized or decentralized anomaly detection processing framework. These nodes are responsible for initial data acquisition, while the central framework aggregates and correlates measurements from across the network.
Data collection follows a continuous streaming or periodic high-frequency sampling paradigm, with electrical measurements transmitted from edge devices and meters to the processing framework. Communication relies on IoT protocols suited for reliable telemetry, such as MQTT and Modbus TCP, ensuring low-latency, real-time data transmission across the network infrastructure.
To protect telemetry data from tampering and ensure data integrity, all communication channels employ encryption mechanisms such as TLS/SSL. This is particularly important given the critical infrastructure context, where compromised data could lead to incorrect operational decisions.
The system generates streams of raw and pre-processed electrical measurements, alongside preliminary anomaly flags or alerts produced by edge-level algorithms. These data streams are forwarded to the comprehension layer, where deeper analysis and mitigation strategy generation take place, forming the foundation for automated or operator-assisted responses to detected grid issues.

#### _**Digital twin for electricity distribution network with distributed generation and flexible load**_ _**(**__**ISEP**__**)**_

The digital twin for the electricity distribution network serves as the foundational analytical engine of the comprehension layer. Its primary purpose is to maintain operational continuity and minimise data loss in the event of communication failures, ensuring that operators always have access to a reliable representation of the grid state regardless of the availability of real-time telemetry.
The digital twin ingests real-time telemetry from smart meters, provided by the perception layer, alongside historical baseline datasets that characterize the expected behavior of the network under various operating conditions. This combination of live and historical data enables the system to build and continuously update a comprehensive model of the physical network.
At its core, the system employs state estimation algorithms and prediction models. State estimation synthesizes available measurements into a coherent picture of the current network state, while prediction models extrapolate forward in time or fill in gaps where sensor data is missing or degraded. The data processing pipeline consists of an ingestion stage that normalises and validates incoming telemetry, feeding into the state estimation engine, which in turn updates the continuous digital representation of the physical distribution network.
The principal outputs of the system are synthesized virtual sensor data, predicted grid states, and the continuously updated digital twin itself. When physical telemetry is lost or degraded due to communication failures, the state prediction algorithms generate virtual sensor readings that maintain the fidelity of the model, preventing information gaps from propagating into downstream decision-making processes.
By providing operators with a reliable and uninterrupted view of the grid state even during communication outages, the digital twin ensures that situational awareness is maintained at all times. This is essential for the energy community use case, where distributed generation and flexible loads introduce variability that must be understood continuously to support safe and efficient grid operation.

#### _**Dashboard for energy community management**_ _**(ISEP)**_

The dashboard for energy community management is a comprehension enabler designed to handle the full lifecycle of community participants, provide transparency for end-users to encourage active participation, and enable comprehensive management capabilities for the Distribution System Operator (DSO). It translates the data acquired by the perception layer into human-interpretable visualizations and reports.
The system consolidates data regarding consumers, production levels, consumption patterns, and system analytics from the perception layer. Data integration is governed by access control policies that ensure each user role sees only the information they are authorized to access.
Architecturally, the system is structured as a backend processing engine that exposes a series of REST API endpoints, which feed aggregated and processed data to a frontend User Interface (UI).
The outputs produced by the system include interactive visual dashboards, automated reports, and continuous tracking of production and consumption trends at both the individual prosumer and the aggregate community levels. Trend tracking and visualization also serve as optimization mechanisms, providing insights that support community performance improvement over time.
From an operational awareness perspective, the dashboard gives operators and the DSO a clear, user-friendly visual overview of the community's current state, fostering transparency and facilitating informed decision-making regarding participant lifecycle management, resource allocation, and community strategy.

#### _**Activation of flexible resources**_ _**(ISEP)**_

The activation of flexible resources enabler bridges the gap between situational comprehension and real-time actuation, closing the control loop within the situational awareness framework. Its purpose is to enable the real-time dispatch and control of flexible resources, specifically targeting battery energy storage systems infrastructure, in response to emergencies, voltage issues, or network overloads detected by the perception and comprehension layers.
The system integrates data from multiple sources within the comprehension layer, processing alerts generated by the anomaly detection mechanisms, grid state information provided by the digital twin, and flexibility availability data describing the current capacity and constraints of each controllable asset. By combining these inputs, the system determines the most appropriate actuation response for each detected event.

The core data processing relies on optimisation and control algorithms that evaluate dynamic response constraints — including asset availability, state of charge, ramp rates, and contractual limitations — to ensure that actuation requirements are met without violating operational or safety boundaries. These algorithms select and schedule the optimal combination of flexible resources to deploy in response to each specific grid condition.

Architecturally, the system is structured as a control decision engine that receives situational alerts from upstream comprehension components, calculates the required flexibility contribution, and issues commands via robust communication interfaces to the deployed assets. The communication layer is designed for reliability and low latency, given the time-critical nature of emergency grid responses.

The primary outputs of the system are real-time actuation commands and dispatch signals sent directly to flexible resources, instructing them to charge, discharge, or modulate their consumption according to the determined mitigation strategy. The optimisation mechanisms continuously evaluate whether the applied response constraints are being met, adjusting commands as needed to ensure that mitigation strategies successfully and safely resolve detected grid issues.

By translating situational comprehension into actionable mitigation steps, this enabler actively stabilises the grid based on the perceived and understood state of the network, completing the awareness-to-action cycle that is central to the Sa4CPS approach.

#### _**Energy Community Situational Comprehension Engine**_ _**(Digitalmente, CEL, ISEP)**_

The Energy Community Situational Comprehension Engine is a collaborative comprehension enabler developed jointly by Digitalmente, CEL, and ISEP. Its purpose is to transform perceived grid, consumption, and flexibility data into actionable situational understanding that empowers energy community operators to act proactively rather than reactively.

The engine aggregates consumption, production, and flexibility signals from the perception layer, integrating data streams from multiple sources into a unified analytical framework. This aggregation spans individual prosumer-level data through to community-wide metrics, providing a multi-scale view of the energy community's operational state.

The data processing methods employed include trend analysis to identify evolving patterns in community behaviour, scenario comparison to evaluate alternative operational strategies, historical analysis to contextualise current conditions against past performance, and KPI computation covering metrics such as self-consumption rates, peak demand reduction, and carbon emissions. Together, these methods transform raw perception data into structured, decision-relevant information.

Architecturally, the system is structured as an ingestion and processing engine that aggregates incoming signals, computes the relevant metrics and indicators, and feeds the results into visualisation tools specifically tailored to CEL operational workflows. This design ensures that the outputs are not only analytically rigorous but also immediately usable by operators in their day-to-day management activities.

The system produces actionable insights, aggregated views across multiple dimensions, KPI reports, and early warnings for abnormal or inefficient states. Specialised anomaly detection algorithms continuously monitor community behaviour patterns, flagging deviations that may indicate equipment faults, unexpected consumption shifts, or opportunities for operational improvement.

By providing CEL operators with a comprehensive, current, and projected view of the energy community, the comprehension engine supports proactive decision-making, operational planning, and scenario evaluation — ultimately enabling the community to operate more efficiently, resiliently, and sustainably.

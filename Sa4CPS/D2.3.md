## 1.1       Critical energy distribution (UC4)

A distribution network operated by DSO, wants to develop the means to use flexibility in case of reliability issues in the distribution grid or when asked by the upstream network operator. At the same time, this operator is managing an energy community composed of a part of the consumers in the area, targeting to optimize the energy contracts definition considering user's flexibility, energy prices, and operation costs. A flexibility aggregator gets involved to collect and provide this flexibility.

Main contribution area to the Sa4CPS concept: Distributed AI/explainable AI, digital twin. Several initiatives in the field focus on energy aspects, ignoring the learning of user's comfort and flexibility.

Targeted innovation boosted by the Sa4CPS concept: Use of Explainable AI (XAI) for situational awareness; Activation of Flexibility with XAI; Use of federated learning models for energy communities. Target business impact: Use of flexibility; Optimization of resource use.

//Deployment architecture: each building has a Raspberry Pi 5 acting as edge gateway. Services are deployed as containers. IoT devices (smart meters, environmental sensors, smart plugs) publish data locally via MQTT broker running on the Pi. All processing and data storage happen locally by default — building data stays within the building unless the user explicitly authorizes sharing.

//Inter-building communication uses Tailscale/Headscale VPN overlay network. Buildings that opt-in to community sharing expose selected data to other buildings or to the community aggregator/DSO. Tailscale provides encrypted peer-to-peer connections without exposing nodes to the public internet.

//Community-level services (aggregation, flexibility coordination, community dashboards) run on a central node accessible through the same Tailscale network. These services only consume data that buildings have explicitly made available.

Figure Y. Deployment architecture of UC4.

//Generic description of a building node: Raspberry Pi 5 runs MQTT broker, data collection agents, local AI models, and a digital twin of the building. IoT devices connect via MQTT. //Data flow within a building: IoT devices publish sensor readings to the local MQTT broker. Local agents consume these readings for monitoring, learning, and actuation. Data remains on-premises unless the building user authorizes sharing with the community.

//Data flow between buildings: when sharing is authorized, selected data is made available over the Tailscale network. Other buildings or the community aggregator can subscribe to this shared data. The central node mediates discovery — once buildings know each other, they communicate peer-to-peer. The DSO or flexibility aggregator accesses community-level data through the same network.

### 1.1.1    Contribution to Tools for Comprehension

//Tools that help users and operators understand what is happening in the building and community

//XAI provides interpretable explanations of energy consumption patterns, comfort preferences, and flexibility potential. Users can understand why certain recommendations or actions are suggested.

//Federated learning models trained across buildings without sharing raw data — each building contributes to community-level models while preserving privacy. Comprehension of community-wide patterns emerges without centralizing sensitive data.

//Dashboards and visualizations present building-level and community-level energy data, comfort metrics, and flexibility status in an accessible way.

### 1.1.2    Contribution to Digital Twins and Dashboards

//Digital twin of each building represents current state: energy consumption, production (if prosumer), occupancy, comfort levels, device status. Updated in real-time from IoT data via MQTT.

//Community-level digital twin aggregates opted-in building data to provide DSO and aggregator with overview of available flexibility, consumption forecasts, and community energy balance.

//Dashboards present operational information: energy prices, flexibility requests from upstream operator, comfort trade-offs, cost optimization opportunities. Operators and users interact with the digital twin through these interfaces to assess current and projected states.

### 1.1.3    Contribution to Detection and Response

//Detection: edge nodes process local data close to the source, enabling low-latency detection of events. Proximity to IoT devices minimizes delay between data acquisition and event identification, which is critical for time-sensitive flexibility activation.

//Flexibility activation: when the upstream operator requests flexibility or when energy prices make it advantageous, local agents on each Pi can activate pre-agreed flexibility measures (shift loads, adjust setpoints) with user consent. Edge-based execution ensures response times are not dependent on network round-trips to central infrastructure.

//Anomaly detection on local energy and comfort data — alerts when consumption deviates from expected patterns or when device malfunctions are detected.

//Automated responses within user-defined boundaries: comfort preferences set limits on what the system can adjust autonomously. Actions beyond those limits require explicit user approval.

//Community-level coordination: aggregator collects flexibility offers from buildings and responds to DSO requests, distributing actions across willing participants.

### 1.1.4    Summary

//UC4 addresses situational awareness in energy communities by enabling each building to perceive its own state through local IoT data, comprehend patterns through XAI and federated learning, and act on flexibility opportunities through edge-based automation. The distributed architecture ensures that awareness is built locally first — each building maintains its own digital twin and processes its own data — and extends to the community level only through explicit user authorization. This layered approach to situational awareness allows the energy community to coordinate flexibility, optimize energy use, and respond to grid-level events while preserving individual building autonomy and data privacy.

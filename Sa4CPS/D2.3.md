## 1.1       Critical energy distribution (UC4)

A distribution network operated by DSO, wants to develop the means to use flexibility in case of reliability issues in the distribution grid or when asked by the upstream network operator. At the same time, this operator is managing an energy community composed of a part of the consumers in the area, targeting to optimize the energy contracts definition considering user's flexibility, energy prices, and operation costs. A flexibility aggregator gets involved to collect and provide this flexibility.

Main contribution area to the Sa4CPS concept: Distributed AI/explainable AI, digital twin. Several initiatives in the field focus on energy aspects, ignoring the learning of user's comfort and flexibility.

Targeted innovation boosted by the Sa4CPS concept: Use of Explainable AI (XAI) for situational awareness; Activation of Flexibility with XAI; Use of federated learning models for energy communities. Target business impact: Use of flexibility; Optimization of resource use.

The UC4 system is structured around an energy community composed of multiple buildings, each operating as an independent edge node. Each building functions autonomously, collecting and processing its own data locally. However, for the buildings to operate as a coordinated energy community, a central node is required to mediate initial contact and service discovery. Once buildings are aware of each other through this central mediator, they can establish direct peer-to-peer communication channels for data exchange and coordination.

Figure X. System level overview of UC4.

The deployment architecture of UC4 follows a distributed edge computing model. Each building is equipped with a Raspberry Pi 5 that acts as the local edge gateway, running all services as containers. IoT devices — including smart meters, environmental sensors, and smart plugs — publish their data to an MQTT broker running locally on the Raspberry Pi. All data processing and storage occur on-premises by default, meaning that building data remains within the building unless the user explicitly authorizes its sharing with the community.

Inter-building communication relies on a Tailscale/Headscale VPN overlay network. Buildings that opt in to community-level data sharing expose selected data to other buildings or to the community aggregator and the DSO. Tailscale establishes encrypted peer-to-peer connections between nodes without exposing them to the public internet, ensuring secure communication across geographically distributed buildings.

Community-level services, such as energy aggregation, flexibility coordination, and community dashboards, run on a central node that is accessible through the same Tailscale network. These services consume only the data that individual buildings have explicitly made available, preserving the principle that data sovereignty remains with each building owner.

Figure Y. Deployment architecture of UC4.

At the building level, the Raspberry Pi 5 serves as the core of the local infrastructure. It hosts the MQTT broker, data collection agents, local AI models, and a digital twin representation of the building. IoT devices connect to the Pi via MQTT, and all local processing — including comfort-related decisions and flexibility activation — is performed on the edge node without depending on external connectivity. This ensures low-latency responses to local events and continued operation even when network connectivity to the community is unavailable.

Within a building, the data flow follows a publish-subscribe pattern. IoT devices publish sensor readings to the local MQTT broker, and local agents consume these readings for monitoring, learning, and actuation purposes. Data remains entirely on-premises unless the building user explicitly authorizes sharing with the community.

When sharing is authorized, the data flow extends beyond the building boundary. Selected data is made available over the Tailscale network, allowing other buildings or the community aggregator to access it. The central node mediates the initial discovery process, enabling buildings to locate and authenticate each other. After discovery, buildings communicate directly in a peer-to-peer fashion. The DSO and the flexibility aggregator access community-level data through the same network, consuming only what has been explicitly shared.

### 1.1.1    Contribution to Tools for Comprehension

In an energy community where buildings operate independently and data is distributed across multiple edge nodes, users and operators need tools that make the current state of the system understandable. UC4 addresses this need through a set of comprehension tools that translate raw IoT data into interpretable insights at both the building and community levels.

Explainable AI (XAI) techniques are employed to provide users with interpretable explanations of energy consumption patterns, comfort preferences, and flexibility potential. Rather than presenting opaque model outputs, XAI surfaces the reasoning behind recommendations and suggested actions, enabling users to understand why a particular flexibility measure is proposed or why a consumption pattern has been flagged. This transparency is essential for building user trust and supporting informed participation in community-level coordination.

Federated learning models are trained across the buildings in the community without requiring the sharing of raw data. Each building contributes to the training of community-level models while keeping its data local, preserving privacy. Through this approach, comprehension of community-wide energy patterns — such as aggregate consumption trends or shared flexibility potential — emerges without centralizing sensitive information. The resulting models enable each building and the community as a whole to better understand collective behaviour while respecting individual data sovereignty.

Dashboards and visualizations complement the AI-driven tools by presenting building-level and community-level energy data, comfort metrics, and flexibility status in an accessible format. These interfaces allow users and operators to quickly assess the state of their building or the community, supporting comprehension without requiring technical expertise in data analysis or machine learning.

### 1.1.2    Contribution to Digital Twins and Dashboards

Effective situational awareness in energy communities requires a continuously updated representation of each building's state and the community's overall condition. UC4 achieves this through digital twin models and operational dashboards that provide real-time visibility into energy, comfort, and flexibility.

Each building maintains a digital twin that represents its current state, including energy consumption, energy production (in the case of prosumers), occupancy levels, comfort conditions, and device status. The digital twin is updated in real time from IoT data received through the local MQTT broker, ensuring that the representation reflects the actual state of the building at any given moment. This local digital twin serves as the foundation for both automated decision support and user-facing interfaces within the building.

At the community level, a digital twin aggregates data from buildings that have opted in to sharing. This aggregated representation provides the DSO and the community aggregator with an overview of available flexibility, consumption forecasts, and the overall energy balance of the community. By combining individual building states into a community-level view, the digital twin enables coordinated planning and response to grid-level events, while only incorporating data that has been explicitly authorized for sharing.

Operational dashboards present decision-relevant information to users and operators, including current energy prices, flexibility requests from the upstream network operator, comfort trade-offs, and cost optimization opportunities. Users interact with the digital twin through these dashboard interfaces, allowing them to assess both current conditions and projected states. This combination of digital twins and dashboards provides the informational basis for decisions at the building and community levels.

### 1.1.3    Contribution to Detection and Response

In energy communities where flexibility activation and anomaly handling require timely intervention, the ability to detect events and respond quickly is critical. UC4 leverages its distributed edge architecture to process data close to the source, minimizing latency between data acquisition and action.

Each edge node processes local IoT data directly on the Raspberry Pi 5, enabling low-latency detection of events within the building. The proximity of the processing node to the IoT devices minimizes the delay between sensor reading acquisition and event identification. This is particularly important for time-sensitive operations such as flexibility activation, where response speed directly affects the value and reliability of the flexibility provided to the grid.

When the upstream network operator requests flexibility or when energy market conditions make flexibility activation advantageous, local agents running on each Raspberry Pi can activate pre-agreed flexibility measures. These measures include load shifting, adjustment of temperature setpoints, and rescheduling of deferrable consumption, all executed with user consent. Because the decision and execution logic runs at the edge, response times are not dependent on network round-trips to central infrastructure, ensuring reliable and timely activation.

Anomaly detection is performed locally on energy and comfort data. The system generates alerts when consumption patterns deviate from expected behaviour or when device malfunctions are identified. Automated responses operate within user-defined boundaries — comfort preferences establish limits on what the system can adjust autonomously, and actions that would exceed those limits require explicit user approval before execution.

At the community level, the flexibility aggregator coordinates across buildings by collecting flexibility offers and responding to DSO requests. The aggregator distributes activation signals to willing participants, enabling the community to respond collectively to grid-level events while respecting the autonomy and preferences of each individual building.

### 1.1.4    Summary

UC4 addresses situational awareness in energy communities through a distributed, privacy-preserving architecture where each building operates as an independent edge node. Situational awareness is constructed from the building level upward: each building perceives its own state through local IoT data collection, comprehends patterns and anomalies through XAI and federated learning, and acts on flexibility opportunities through edge-based automation with minimal latency. The architecture extends awareness to the community level only through explicit user authorization, ensuring that data sovereignty is maintained. This layered approach enables the energy community to coordinate flexibility, optimize energy use, and respond to grid-level events while preserving the autonomy and privacy of each participating building.
